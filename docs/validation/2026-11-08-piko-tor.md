Limits of these measurements:

- The direct IPv6 positive control failed with "Address family unavailable".
  Successful IPv6 through Tor and zero IPv6 counters do not prove absence of direct
  IPv6 leakage on an underlying network with working IPv6.
- UID output counters may not attribute DNS sent by Android's `netd`; DNS can also
  be cached. The results are not exhaustive DNS-leak evidence.
- UDP was a synthetic host echo, not a real QUIC implementation.
- The probe was an independent app, not authenticated X or its auxiliary processes.
  Emulator results are not a physical-device/OEM compatibility matrix.

Earlier attempts failed preflight or direct positive controls and were not passing
network evidence. The successful run followed corrections to the disposable-emulator
preflight and probe readiness; it does not turn the earlier failures into evidence
of blocking.

## VPN behavior and remaining limits

Android permits one active VPN per profile. In the tested **unpublished development
source**, before requesting consent, the companion checks for another VPN visible
to its UID and refuses to proceed if one
is found. **This is a best-effort guard:** Android can hide per-app VPNs that exclude
the companion. The user must inspect Android's VPN settings before starting; the
guard does not guarantee detection or preservation of every other VPN provider.

After the consent dialog, the companion rechecks its permission and selected target
before starting. If another provider was selected in the meantime, a stale success
result does not trigger a new consent request or start the service. Revocation
invalidates callbacks before queued main-thread cleanup can release resources.
Unit tests cover these code paths; real provider-switching and OEM behavior still
require device validation.

The **published 3.9.0-tor.2 companion lacks the visible-VPN guard and post-consent
recheck** described here. The existing download does not include these development
safeguards.

Strict operation requires manually enabled **Always-on VPN** and **Block connections
without VPN** for Piko Tor. Per-app lockdown can block other apps in the profile.
Revoking consent, changing provider, disabling lockdown or uninstalling the companion
can remove that system barrier. No current result certifies exhaustive prevention
of DNS, IPv4/IPv6, UDP/QUIC, WebView, native or isolated-process leaks.

Not demonstrated by this session: authenticated X traffic through Tor; full
physical-device network behavior of patched X and the companion; login, media, direct messages and
notifications; reboot/early-boot traffic; Wi-Fi/mobile transitions; background/battery
restrictions; longer automatic-restart observation; and exhaustive negative tests
for other engine failures, service death and policy/provider changes.

The [October 19 observations](../../companion/README.md#historical-android-runtime-checks-2026-10-19)
remain historical evidence for revision `4602d80a`. They include unsuccessful
automatic recovery after process death and an unattributed physical DNS exchange.
They neither certify this development version nor resolve those earlier limits.
